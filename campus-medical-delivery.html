Campus Medical Delivery App

Introduction to Management · Rutgers Business School

A business concept for on-demand medicine delivery to Rutgers students, with a process map, performance measures, and an improvement plan built around customer satisfaction and delivery cost.

TimelineJan 2026 – May 2026
Team4 students
TypeConcept and case project
ToolsSIPOC, performance charts, SMART goals

The Concept

Students who get sick late at night or during exams often have no quick way to get basic medical supplies. Our concept was a mobile app that delivers items like pain relievers, bandages, and allergy medicine to dorms and apartments, fulfilled from campus pop-up shops and delivered by student workers. We built a go-to-market plan, then treated the delivery service as a business process to map, measure, and improve.

Performance numbers on this page are illustrative data created for the course exercise, since the service was a concept and not a live business.

Process Map (SIPOC)

We started from the customer and worked backward to map the full delivery process.

For customers, we defined the behaviors we wanted (repeat orders, positive reviews, referrals, app engagement) and the requirements needed to earn them (late-night speed, accurate orders, affordable pricing, a simple app).

We built "well-dressed measure" charts that show performance against prior periods, a competitor, and a best-in-class benchmark, along with a goal, status, owner, and active improvement project.

Loyalty and cost were both rated "needs improvement." Repeat customers were growing each quarter but trailed the competitor, and costs were falling but stayed above competitors. We tied these gaps to delivery delays, inconsistent service, inefficient driver scheduling, and supplier costs.

Improvement Initiative

Goal: raise customer satisfaction from 4.6 to 4.8 by September 2026

We wrote the goal as a SMART goal: a specific score, a measurable target of 4.8, an achievable +0.2 gain, directly tied to customer experience, with a September 2026 deadline.

We proposed five changes to the delivery process:

  • Real-time delivery tracking so customers know when their order will arrive.
  • Better inventory forecasting to keep high-demand items in stock.
  • Standardized runner training for consistent service quality.
  • Automatic follow-up and check-up messages after each delivery.
  • Demand-based staffing and pop-up shop hours, expanded during flu season and high-demand weeks.

What I Took Away

  • How to map a service as a process and define it from the customer's point of view.
  • How to choose effectiveness and efficiency measures and benchmark them against competitors.
  • How to turn a performance gap into a specific initiative with a measurable goal, which is the same logic used in inventory and operations management.
